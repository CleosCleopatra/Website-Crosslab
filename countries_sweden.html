Sweden is a Nordic country in Northern Europe, sharing borders with Norway, Finland and Denmark. The country has dramatical differences in climate both within the country and throughout the year. During the winders the weather is generally cold, with the North being colder, while summers are generally milder, especially in the South. In the Northern part of Sweden, temperatures as low as -53 celsius have been recorded.

The difference in daylight is equally extreme. With summers leading to long days, especially in the North where the sun may not set for around 50 days. In the winter meanwhile, the North can go around a month without the sun rising.
Much of Swedens population live in the bigger cities of the South, while the Northern portion of the country has more forest and less people. The country is known for engineering, design and innovation, with companies such as Volvo, IKEA, Spotify and Minecraft having Swedish roots.

Good to know when working together

  1. Tip 1

    Discussion, Discussion, Discussion

    Swedish teams may spend a lot of time discussing different options before deciding on a direction to ensure everyone agrees with the chosen solution. This can make decisions take a little longer.

  2. Tip 2

    Punctionality matters

    Meetings are generally expected to start on time. If you think you'll be late to a meeting or miss a deadline, let your teammates know as early as possible.

  3. Tip 3

    Independence is valued

    Students are usually expected to plan their own work and take responsibility for their tasks themselves, rather than waiting for detailed instructions. Asking for help when you need it is still encouraged.

  4. Tip 4

    Hierarchies are less formal

    Swedish study and work environments can be informal. First names are used, even with teachers or the prime minister and everyone is enocuraged to voice their opinion, regardless of their role.

  5. Tip 5

    Ideas can be challenged

    Swedes may openly suggest alternatives or question a proposed solution, even when it comes from someone more experienced or in a leadership role. This is often about understanding and improving the idea, rather than criticising the person.

  6. Tip 6

    Fika!

    Fika is a Swedish tradition of taking a drink coffee or tea break, often with a snack and other people. It is a chance to take a break from work and socialise and is done daily at most places of work in Sweden. Crosslab will have a Fika every session.
